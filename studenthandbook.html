INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Preamble

The PhilTechGMA Student Handbook serves as the official codification of policies, standards, and student rights. It is designed to uphold the institution's commitment to academic excellence, discipline, and holistic formation. All students enrolled in any program are bound by the provisions herein. The handbook is complemented by the Violation Monitoring & Suspension Decision Support System to ensure consistent, fair, and data-driven implementation of disciplinary measures.

This handbook is effective from the date of issuance and supersedes all previous versions. Any amendments shall be approved by the College President upon recommendation of the Student Discipline Committee.

Last revised: August 2025 | Approved by: Office of Student Affairs
CODE OF CONDUCT

General Policies & Student Responsibilities

  • Attendance & Punctuality: Students must attend all scheduled classes and official activities. Tardiness exceeding 15 minutes is recorded as absence. A student with 20% unexcused absences per subject shall be subject to probation.
  • Uniform & Identification: The official school uniform and ID must be worn at all times inside the campus. Lost IDs must be reported within 24 hours; temporary ID is required.
  • Classroom Decorum: Respectful interaction, no use of gadgets for non-academic purposes during class, and adherence to academic integrity are mandatory.
  • Campus Property: Vandalism, theft, or unauthorized use of facilities will result in sanctions including restitution and suspension.
  • Substance-Free Campus: Possession, use, or distribution of alcohol, tobacco, or illegal drugs is a grave offense with immediate hearing and potential expulsion.
  • Anti-Bullying & Harassment: Any form of physical, verbal, cyber, or sexual harassment is strictly prohibited and shall be investigated with strict confidentiality.
CLASSIFICATION OF VIOLATIONS

Categories of Offenses

Offenses are classified based on severity, impact on community, and recurrence. The Suspension Decision Support System uses a point-based system: Class A (1 pt), Class B (3 pts), Class C (6 pts). Accumulation of 5 points within a semester triggers automatic review.

Class A – Minor Offenses

Typically result in warnings, reflection, or community service. No suspension unless repeated within the same semester.

  • Habitual tardiness (3+ incidents/month)
  • Improper uniform / ID not displayed
  • Littering / eating in non-designated areas
  • Use of gadgets during class (non-academic)
  • Failure to observe queue or corridor noise

Class B – Serious Offenses

Probation, parental conference, suspension (1–5 days). Record remains active for two semesters.

  • Cheating / plagiarism (first offense)
  • Unauthorized absence (cutting classes)
  • Verbal harassment / disrespect to faculty or staff
  • Vandalism / minor property damage
  • Falsification of documents (first offense)
  • Unauthorized use of school Wi-Fi for prohibited activities

Class C – Grave Offenses

Immediate suspension, hearing, and possible expulsion. Permanent record annotation.

  • Physical assault / severe bullying
  • Cheating (repeat offense) or large-scale plagiarism
  • Possession or distribution of prohibited substances
  • Cyber misconduct, hacking, or identity theft
  • Sexual harassment or acts of lewdness
  • Tampering with institutional records or systems

COMPUTER STUDIES DEPARTMENT

CS Department Specific Policies

Laboratory & Network Integrity
  • Unauthorized software/hardware: Installing unlicensed software, tampering with hardware, or bypassing security protocols is a Class B offense (first offense).
  • Network abuse: Attempting to hack, access restricted systems, or using VPN to bypass filters results in Class B or C depending on intent.
  • Food & drinks inside labs: Strictly prohibited; violation is Class A with community service.
  • Data privacy breach: Accessing or sharing confidential data without permission is a Class C offense.
Academic Integrity & Capstone
  • Code plagiarism: First offense – grade of zero on the requirement + mandatory ethics seminar. Repeat offense – Class B suspension (5 days) and notation on record.
  • AI misuse: Submission of AI-generated work without disclosure is treated as plagiarism.
  • Internship/Capstone attendance: Unexcused absences beyond 20% of total hours results in automatic probation and possible course failure.
  • Collaboration violations: Unauthorized sharing of code or answers during exams is a Class B offense.
Monitoring & Reporting

All CS faculty are required to submit incident reports via the Violation Monitoring System within 24 hours. The system aggregates violations and recommends intervention programs (e.g., ethics seminars, counseling) to promote restorative justice.

FAIRNESS & ACCOUNTABILITY

Student Rights & Disciplinary Process

Every student is entitled to due process, which includes the right to be informed of the charges, to present evidence, and to appeal. The process follows these steps:

1. Incident Report

Filed by faculty/staff within 24h

2. Adviser Review

Initial assessment & counseling

3. Committee Hearing

Formal deliberation with student

4. Sanction & Appeal

3-day appeal window to Dean

Right to Appeal: Students may submit a written appeal to the Office of Student Affairs within three (3) working days from receipt of the decision. The decision of the College President after review is final.

ACADEMIC STANDING

Policy on Retention & Good Standing

To maintain enrollment, students must meet the following academic retention standards:

  • Maintain a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of at least 2.00 (or equivalent passing grade for all courses).
  • No more than two (2) failing grades in a semester; otherwise, probation with prescribed remedial plan.
  • Students on academic probation who fail to meet required GPA after one semester may be dismissed from the program.
  • Disciplinary probation due to violations may affect eligibility for scholarships, leadership roles, and graduation honors.

The Violation Monitoring System integrates academic and disciplinary standing to provide a holistic student record.

REFERENCE

Glossary of Terms

Class A/B/C: Classification of offenses based on severity.

Suspension Decision Support System: Digital tool that recommends sanctions based on violation history and category.

Probation: A period of monitored conduct with specific conditions; further violation may lead to suspension.

Community Service: Non-academic duties assigned as restorative sanction (e.g., campus cleanup, assisting in events).

Incident Report: Formal documentation of a violation submitted by a faculty or staff member.

Due Process: The right to a fair hearing, notice of charges, and opportunity to respond before any sanction is finalized.
