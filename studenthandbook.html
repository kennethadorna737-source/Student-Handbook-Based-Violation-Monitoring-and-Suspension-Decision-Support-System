INSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

Preamble

The PhilTechGMA Student Handbook constitutes the official codification of rights, responsibilities, and academic standards governing the student body. It reflects the institution’s unwavering commitment to intellectual excellence, ethical conduct, and holistic formation. All enrolled students, regardless of program or year level, are bound by the provisions herein. The handbook is operationally integrated with the Violation Monitoring & Suspension Decision Support System, ensuring consistent, equitable, and data-informed application of disciplinary measures.

This edition supersedes all prior versions and takes effect upon issuance. Amendments require approval by the College President following recommendation from the Student Discipline Committee. Interpretation of policies resides with the Office of Student Affairs.

Revision: August 2025 | Approved by: Office of Student Affairs & Academic Council
CODE OF CONDUCT & STUDENT RESPONSIBILITIES

General Policies & Institutional Expectations

  • Attendance and Punctuality: Regular attendance in all classes and academic activities is mandatory. Tardiness beyond fifteen (15) minutes shall be recorded as an absence. Accumulation of unexcused absences exceeding 20% of total class sessions subjects the student to academic probation and possible exclusion.
  • Uniform, Identification, and Decorum: The official school uniform and valid ID must be visibly worn within campus premises at all times. Lost identification cards must be reported within twenty-four (24) hours; a temporary ID is required pending replacement.
  • Classroom Conduct and Academic Integrity: Respectful discourse, prohibition of non-academic gadget use during instruction, and adherence to academic honesty standards are strictly enforced.
  • Preservation of Campus Property: Vandalism, theft, unauthorized entry, or misuse of institutional facilities will result in sanctions including restitution, suspension, and possible expulsion.
  • Substance-Free Environment: Possession, consumption, distribution, or being under the influence of alcohol, tobacco, or illegal substances constitutes a grave offense warranting immediate hearing and potential expulsion.
  • Anti-Bullying and Harassment: Any form of physical, verbal, cyber, or sexual harassment is prohibited. Complaints are investigated with strict confidentiality and procedural fairness.
CLASSIFICATION OF VIOLATIONS

Categories of Offenses

Offenses are categorized by severity, impact on the academic community, and recurrence. The Suspension Decision Support System employs a weighted point system: Class A (1 point), Class B (3 points), and Class C (6 points). Accumulation of five (5) points within a single semester triggers an automatic disciplinary review.

Category A

Generally result in corrective measures such as warnings, reflective essays, or community service. Sanctions escalate only upon repetition within the same semester.

  • Repeated tardiness (three or more incidents per month)
  • Improper wearing of uniform or failure to display ID
  • Littering or consuming food in non-designated zones
  • Unauthorized use of electronic devices during class hours
  • Disruptive behavior in corridors or academic buildings

Category B

Carry probationary status, mandatory parental conferences, and suspension ranging from one (1) to five (5) days. Records remain active for two semesters.

  • Cheating or plagiarism (first documented instance)
  • Unauthorized absence from class (cutting)
  • Verbal harassment or disrespect toward faculty/staff
  • Vandalism resulting in minor property damage
  • Falsification of academic or admission documents (first offense)
  • Misuse of institutional network resources

Category C

Warrant immediate preventive suspension, formal hearing, and possible expulsion. Permanent annotation on academic records.

  • Physical assault, severe bullying, or hazing
  • Repeat offense of cheating or large-scale plagiarism
  • Possession, distribution, or use of prohibited substances
  • Cyber misconduct, unauthorized system access, or identity theft
  • Sexual harassment, acts of lewdness, or coercion
  • Tampering with institutional records or information systems

COLLEGE OF COMPUTER STUDIES — ADDITIONAL PROVISIONS

Department-Specific Policies

Laboratory & Network Integrity
  • Unauthorized software/hardware modification: Installation of unlicensed software, hardware tampering, or circumvention of security protocols is classified as a Class B offense for first occurrence.
  • Network abuse and security violations: Attempts to compromise institutional systems, unauthorized access, or use of anonymizing services to bypass content filters may be elevated to Class C depending on intent and damage.
  • Laboratory sanitation policy: Food and beverages are strictly prohibited inside computing laboratories; violation constitutes a Class A infraction with assigned community service.
  • Data privacy and confidentiality: Unauthorized access, disclosure, or misuse of institutional or third-party data is a Class C offense subject to expulsion.
Academic Integrity & Capstone Projects
  • Code plagiarism: First proven case results in a grade of zero for the assessed work plus mandatory attendance in an ethics seminar. Repetition triggers Class B suspension (5 days) and permanent annotation.
  • AI-generated content misuse: Submission of AI-generated work without explicit disclosure and instructor consent is treated as academic dishonesty equivalent to plagiarism.
  • Internship and capstone attendance: Unexcused absences exceeding 20% of required hours will lead to automatic probation and possible course failure.
  • Unauthorized collaboration: Sharing code, examination answers, or project deliverables without authorization is a Class B offense.
Monitoring and Reporting Protocols

Faculty members and staff are required to submit incident reports via the Violation Monitoring System within twenty-four (24) hours of knowledge. The system aggregates violation data and recommends restorative interventions such as ethics seminars, counseling, or peer mentorship to support behavioral reform.

PROCEDURAL FAIRNESS & DUE PROCESS

Student Rights and Disciplinary Procedure

All students are entitled to due process, including the right to receive written notice of charges, access to evidence, representation by an adviser, and the opportunity to appeal. The disciplinary process follows these sequential stages:

1. Incident Report

Filed by faculty/staff within 24h

2. Adviser Review

Preliminary assessment & guidance

3. Committee Hearing

Formal deliberation with respondent

4. Decision & Appeal

3-day appeal period to Dean/OSA

Right to Appeal: A student may submit a written appeal to the Office of Student Affairs within three (3) working days from receipt of the final decision. The College President’s ruling after review is considered final and executory.

ACADEMIC STANDING & CONTINUING ENROLLMENT

Retention Policy and Good Standing

To maintain enrollment eligibility, students must satisfy the following academic retention criteria:

  • Maintain a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of at least 2.00 (or its equivalent passing standard) across all courses.
  • No more than two (2) failing grades in any given semester; otherwise, mandatory probation with a structured remedial plan.
  • Students placed on academic probation who fail to meet the required CGPA after one semester may be dismissed from the program.
  • Disciplinary probation may affect eligibility for scholarships, leadership appointments, graduation honors, and international mobility programs.

The Violation Monitoring System consolidates academic and disciplinary records to generate a holistic student profile, enabling proactive advising and intervention.

DEFINITIONS & REFERENCE

Glossary of Terms

Class A / B / C Offenses: Classification system based on the gravity of the violation, affecting sanction severity and point assignment.

Suspension Decision Support System: Digital platform that assists discipline committees in recommending sanctions using historical data and offense weighting.

Probation (Academic/Disciplinary): A monitored period during which the student must meet specific conditions; further violations may result in suspension or dismissal.

Community Service: Restorative action involving non-academic duties (e.g., campus upkeep, event assistance) designed to reinforce accountability.

Incident Report: Official documentation of an alleged violation submitted by a faculty member, staff, or student witness.

Due Process: The procedural right to notice of charges, a fair hearing, and the opportunity to respond before any final sanction is imposed.
